Field guide

From sherd on the table to a drawing you can trust, and what to do when the tool says REVIEW.

1 · Before you shoot

  • Pick sherds that suit the automatic lane. Best: complete or large vessels, and rim sherds showing about a quarter of the circle or more. Small pieces and body sherds can try the experimental normals_refine axis; send anything it flags, and decorated fine ware, to manual review or the GigaMesh station.
  • Set up the rig once. Light tent with two identical 6500 K lamps at fixed distance and angle, a matte turntable marked in 10° steps. Use cross-polarised light for slipped or glossy ware.
  • Lock the camera. Lowest ISO, white balance set manually (never Auto), zoom fixed for the whole session, 2-second timer or remote.
  • Grey card first. Shoot an 18% grey card at the start of every session; check R=G=B and match the recorded brightness before placing the sherd.

Record your rig and values on Hardware & inputs.

2 · Scale markers

All metric accuracy comes from here. Print 6–10 matte ArUco markers (3–5 cm). Put four flat around the sherd and two on angled card tents so they stay visible at high elevation. Measure distances between marker pairs with a digital calliper and record them in data/reference/marker-measurements.csv.

3 · Capture

  1. Photograph a legible sherd ID first.
  2. Three elevation rings, camera fixed per ring, turntable turned 10° each shot: low (~15–20°), mid (~45°), high (~70°), then 1–3 overhead shots. About 80–150 images.
  3. Keep 60–70% overlap. If reconstruction fails for lack of overlap, redo at 5° steps.
  4. If you want wall thickness, make both the inner and outer surfaces visible. An exterior-only scan gives a single outline and no thickness.
  5. For the underside, prop the sherd about 45° on clay and shoot a second session, then merge.

4 · Reconstruct

Run photogrammetry on your chosen path, set scale from the marker distances, mask the background, and export an OBJ or PLY in millimetres. Save the unedited export as sherd_ID_raw.obj and never overwrite it.

5 · Make the drawing

Desktop app: open Archaemetry, run Self-test once, then choose the mesh, type the sherd ID, say whether it is a rim sherd, a body sherd, a complete vessel or already oriented, pick the mesh units, and click Make drawing. A 1–3 million triangle scan takes about a minute.

Command line:

pip install ./cpu
archaemetry selftest
archaemetry run sherd.ply -o out --id SAL_1.1

Outputs, named by sherd ID: _drawing.svg, .pdf and .png at 1:1, _qa.png (check plot: every point, the cut, thickness), _report.json and _profile.json.

7 · Quick sanity checks

  • Is the scale bar plausible against the physical sherd and the calliper?
  • In the overlay, is the outline smooth apart from real features? Spikes usually mean leftover background or a bad axis.
  • Is the rim radius sensible for the vessel type?

8 · Archive

Keep RAW files forever if the camera has them. The unedited _raw.obj is the 3D equivalent of a site photograph. Share via Zenodo (free DOI) or Sketchfab. Default licence for captured data: CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 unless the museum specifies otherwise.
